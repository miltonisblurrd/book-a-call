Yes. Web and product design covers the interfaces your users and teams actually live in — not just landing pages. That includes SaaS dashboards, authenticated web apps, internal tools, and marketing sites that need to match the in-app experience. Good product design plans for real states, not just the happy path.

## What I design

**Customer-facing product**
- Web apps and SaaS dashboards with clear information hierarchy
- Onboarding, settings, billing, and core workflow screens
- Marketing and product landing pages that feel like one brand

**Internal and operational tools**
- Admin panels, reporting views, and ops dashboards
- Data-heavy layouts that stay scannable under load
- Role-based UI patterns when different users see different states

## Why dashboard design needs discipline

Dashboards fail when everything competes for attention. Good product design prioritizes the job each screen needs to do, uses consistent components, and plans for density without clutter. Empty, loading, error, and permission states are part of the deliverable — because that is where users decide whether your product feels professional.
