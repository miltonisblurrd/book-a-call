A good handoff has clear components, states, responsive behavior, and notes for edge cases. I keep files organized and annotate interactions so developers can build without guessing — or sending twenty Slack messages about spacing.

## What developers receive

**Organized Figma files**
- Components with variants for states: default, hover, active, disabled, error
- Responsive layouts with breakpoint notes — not just a desktop mockup
- Consistent naming, grouping, and page structure devs can navigate quickly

**Build-ready details**
- Spacing, typography, and color values tied to a design system
- Interaction notes for transitions, modals, and scroll behavior
- Empty, loading, and error states — not just the happy path
- Export specs and redlines where pixel-level precision matters
- A walkthrough call or Loom video when the flow is complex

## Why this matters

Bad handoffs create rework. Developers rebuild screens, make inconsistent UI decisions, and launch products that look nothing like the design. Clean handoff saves weeks of back-and-forth and gets you to launch faster.
