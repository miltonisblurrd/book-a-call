I start by mapping your closest competitors and the brands your customers compare you to — even if they are not direct rivals. Branding without competitive context often produces work that looks fine in isolation but blends in completely once a buyer opens five tabs. The goal is familiar enough to convert and different enough to remember.

## What competitor research covers

**Pattern mapping**
- Repeated visual choices: color palettes, typography, layout tropes
- Messaging clichés everyone in the category uses
- Positioning gaps no one is owning clearly

**Strategic outputs**
- What to avoid so you do not accidentally look like a clone
- One to two category conventions worth following for trust
- One to two differentiation opportunities worth building the brand around

**Applied to design direction**
- Visual and verbal territories tested against the competitive set
- Differentiation tied to real audience needs, not novelty for its own sake
- Documentation so future marketing does not drift back toward sameness

The outcome is a direction grounded in the market — not a mood board disconnected from what buyers actually see when they evaluate you.
