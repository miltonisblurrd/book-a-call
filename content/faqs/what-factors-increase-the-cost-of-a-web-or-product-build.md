Cost increases when scope gets complex, and I clarify those drivers during discovery so there are no surprises after we start. Understanding what moves the number helps you invest in the right places.

## Common cost drivers

**Scope and complexity**
- More screens, flows, or user roles mean more design and build time
- Custom interactions, animations, or data visualizations add engineering depth
- Deeper CMS needs with structured content types and editor workflows

**Integrations and infrastructure**
- Auth, billing, analytics, and third-party APIs each expand front-end and back-end work
- Multi-language, multi-tenant, or enterprise permission models
- Migration from a legacy stack with redirect and SEO requirements

**Timeline pressure**
- Compressed schedules often require parallel workstreams or reduced iteration cycles
- Rush fees apply when launch dates are fixed and scope is still moving

Clear scope keeps cost predictable. I will tell you honestly when something is out of budget and suggest a phased approach so you can still launch on time.
