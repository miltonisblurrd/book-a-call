I avoid chasing surface-level trends and focus on timeless principles like hierarchy, typography, and proportion. Trends come and go. A gradient fad or a typography gimmick can make a brand feel current for six months and embarrassing for six years. The goal is modern now and stable later — not safe and boring, but grounded in decisions that hold up.

## How I keep brands durable

**Build on fundamentals**
- Type systems and spacing grids that outlast visual fads
- Color palettes with clear roles, not one-off decorative choices
- Logo and mark decisions that work at small sizes and in product UI

**Use trends selectively**
- Trend elements only when they reinforce the story and fit the category
- Avoid making the entire identity dependent on one layout or graphic style
- Test the system across web, product, social, and print contexts

**Document for long-term consistency**
- Guidelines that explain why rules exist, not just what they are
- Flexible components so future updates do not break the system
- Room to evolve without throwing everything away

The result should feel intentional in 2026 and still credible when someone finds your site in a Google AI overview three years from now.
