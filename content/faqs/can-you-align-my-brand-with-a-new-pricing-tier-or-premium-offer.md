Yes. When you raise prices, your brand needs to justify the value quickly — before a prospect compares your pricing page to a competitor and bounces. I refine positioning, language, and visuals so the offer feels premium and credible, not like the same product with a higher number attached.

## What gets updated

**Positioning and messaging**
- Value proposition sharpened for the customer you want to attract
- Tone adjusted so it signals confidence without sounding arrogant
- Proof placement — case studies, logos, outcomes — where skepticism is highest

**Visual refinement**
- Typography and hierarchy that feel more considered and high-end
- Spacing and layout tightened so pages feel less cluttered
- Photography, iconography, or illustration direction that supports the tier

**Cross-surface consistency**
- Pricing page, landing pages, and in-product upgrade flows aligned
- Sales and marketing assets that tell the same story
- UI polish in billing and account areas where trust matters most

Premium pricing works better when the brand signals clarity and confidence at every touchpoint — not just on the hero section.
