A headless website separates the front end from the content or commerce backend. I recommend it when you need speed, design flexibility, or custom UX that goes beyond what a standard CMS theme can deliver.

## How headless works

**The split**
- Content lives in a CMS or commerce platform (Sanity, Contentful, Shopify)
- Your front end is built with a modern framework like Next.js or Astro
- Content is delivered via API — your site pulls what it needs, when it needs it

**When it makes sense**
- You want a fully custom design without theme limitations
- Performance matters: static generation, edge caching, and fast page loads
- Content needs to power multiple channels — web, app, email, or print
- Your team needs a clean editing experience without touching code

**When it might be overkill**
- A simple brochure site with rarely updated content
- Tight budget and timeline where a Webflow or WordPress site would suffice

## Why this matters

Headless gives you control over both sides: how content is managed and how the site looks and performs. It is the right choice when a template-based CMS would compromise your brand or product experience.
