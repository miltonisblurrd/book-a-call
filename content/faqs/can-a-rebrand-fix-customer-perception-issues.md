Yes — when the problem is clarity and credibility, not product quality. If customers undervalue what you do, confuse you with competitors, or do not take you seriously because the brand feels outdated, a rebrand can reset perception by aligning how you look with how good you actually are.

## When a rebrand helps

**Perception gaps**
- Your product delivers but your brand looks like an early prototype
- You have outgrown a DIY identity from the founding days
- Enterprise buyers hesitate because the brand does not match the price point

**Strategic shifts**
- You have pivoted, expanded categories, or entered new markets
- The name, messaging, or visual language no longer reflects the product
- A merger or acquisition requires a unified identity

## When a rebrand alone is not enough

If the product has fundamental issues — broken onboarding, poor support, or a mismatch between promise and delivery — no amount of branding fixes retention. Strategy comes first; visuals follow.
