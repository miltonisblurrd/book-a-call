If priorities shift, I pause and reassess scope and timeline before anyone keeps building in the wrong direction. Change is normal — especially for startups and product teams reacting to user feedback, funding, or launch windows. The problem is not the pivot. The problem is when scope creep quietly kills quality because nobody updated the plan.

## How I handle mid-project changes

**Pause and map the impact**
- Identify what can be swapped without losing momentum
- Separate must-haves from nice-to-haves for this phase
- Flag what needs to move to a later sprint or phase

**Update scope and expectations**
- Revise timeline if the new direction adds real work
- Adjust pricing when scope grows beyond the original agreement
- Document decisions so the whole team stays aligned

## Why structure matters

Flexibility works when it is handled transparently. A structured reassessment protects the work already done, keeps stakeholders honest about tradeoffs, and prevents the project from drifting into an undefined rebuild.
