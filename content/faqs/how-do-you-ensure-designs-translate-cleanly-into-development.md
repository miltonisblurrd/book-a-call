I design with development constraints in mind so what you approve in Figma is what ships in production. Clean translation reduces bugs, rework, and the back-and-forth that slows launches.

## How I keep handoff tight

**Components, not one-offs**
- Reusable patterns with consistent naming, spacing, and variants
- Layouts that scale across breakpoints without magic numbers
- No bespoke screens that are impossible to replicate at speed

**States and behavior documented**
- Hover, focus, error, empty, and loading states defined upfront
- Interaction notes for animations, transitions, and edge cases
- Responsive rules annotated so nothing gets guessed in code

**Walkthroughs when complexity warrants it**
- A short Loom or live session for flows with conditional logic
- A handoff checklist covering tokens, assets, and open questions
- Direct access in Slack for quick clarifications during build

When design and dev speak the same language, projects move faster and the final product feels closer to the vision. That is especially important on tight timelines where there is no room for guesswork in implementation.
