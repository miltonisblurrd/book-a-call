I design identity with application in mind, not as a standalone logo exercise. A brand that looks great on a pitch deck but falls apart inside a dashboard is not a system — it is decoration. The goal is one visual language that works on your marketing site, in your product, and in the screenshots users share.

## What makes identity work in both contexts

**Typography and spacing**
- Type scales that read well on long-form pages and dense UI
- Spacing rhythm that translates into real component layouts
- Hierarchy rules that hold up in hero sections and data tables

**Color and contrast**
- Palette usage defined for marketing backgrounds and product surfaces
- Accessible contrast ratios tested in both light and dark contexts
- Semantic color roles so states like success and error stay consistent

**Visual language beyond the logo**
- Icon style, illustration direction, and photography rules
- Component-friendly patterns for buttons, cards, and navigation
- Screenshot-ready layouts that reinforce the brand in the wild

When identity and UI align, your brand feels like one company everywhere — not a polished site attached to a generic app.
