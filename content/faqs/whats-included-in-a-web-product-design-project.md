Product design is not a pretty mockup in isolation. It is a usable system of screens, flows, and decisions your team can build from.

## What you receive

**Discovery + direction**
- Alignment on users, jobs-to-be-done, and success metrics
- Audit of existing product UI if you are redesigning
- Flow mapping for the highest-impact paths first

**UI/UX design**
- High-fidelity screens for web apps, dashboards, or marketing product pages
- Empty, loading, error, and edge states — not just happy paths
- Responsive layouts planned for real breakpoints

**Prototypes + handoff**
- Clickable prototypes for stakeholder review and user testing
- Organized Figma files with components, styles, and naming devs can follow
- Notes on interaction, spacing, and behavior where it matters

## Why this matters for product teams

When design and engineering work from different assumptions, you get rework: rebuilt screens, inconsistent components, and launches that feel half-finished. Build-ready design reduces that friction and gets you to user feedback faster.
