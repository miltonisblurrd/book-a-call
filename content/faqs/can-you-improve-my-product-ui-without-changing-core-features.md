Yes. I can elevate UI through better hierarchy, spacing, typography, and interaction states without touching your feature set. Many products feel rough not because the functionality is wrong, but because the interface makes simple tasks harder than they need to be. Polish the experience first and you often learn what actually needs to be built next.

## What typically gets improved

**Layout and hierarchy**
- Screens that create the most friction or confusion get priority
- Primary actions become obvious; secondary noise gets reduced
- Information density tuned so users scan instead of hunt

**Visual consistency**
- Component styling aligned across flows that grew organically
- Typography, spacing, and color applied with a system mindset
- Empty, loading, and hover states that feel intentional

**Interaction polish**
- Form validation and feedback that guides instead of frustrates
- Transitions and micro-interactions that signal responsiveness
- Mobile layouts that do not feel like an afterthought

You get a better experience without a full rebuild — and a clearer picture of where deeper product changes would actually pay off.
