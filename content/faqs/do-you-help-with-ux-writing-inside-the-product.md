Yes. UX writing is part of product design, not an afterthought. The words inside your product — button labels, error messages, onboarding steps, empty states — directly affect whether users complete tasks or bounce in frustration.

## Where UX writing shows up

**Onboarding and setup**
- Step-by-step copy that reduces anxiety and sets expectations
- Progress indicators and confirmation messages that build confidence
- Tooltips and helper text for non-obvious features

**Core product flows**
- Button labels that describe the action, not generic "Submit" or "Continue"
- Form field labels, placeholders, and validation messages
- Empty states that guide users toward their first action

**Error and edge cases**
- Error messages that explain what went wrong and how to fix it
- System messages for loading, success, and failure states
- Copy for permissions, limits, and account-related notifications

## How I approach it

I keep language consistent with your brand tone so the product feels cohesive — clear and direct, not overly clever. Small words have outsized impact on task completion and user trust.
