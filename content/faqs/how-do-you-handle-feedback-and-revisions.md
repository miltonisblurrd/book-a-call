I set review checkpoints and limit revisions per phase so decisions stay clean. When feedback is specific and tied to goals, quality improves and timelines stay tight — instead of dragging through open-ended revision cycles.

## How the process works

**Structured review rounds**
- Feedback collected at defined milestones — not ad hoc throughout the build
- A set number of revision rounds per phase, agreed upfront
- Consolidated feedback from stakeholders before I start changes

**Feedback that helps**
- Specific: "The CTA is hard to find on mobile" — not "I don't love it"
- Tied to goals: conversion, clarity, brand alignment, or usability
- Prioritized: must-fix vs. nice-to-have so scope stays clear

**When priorities shift**
- Scope changes are handled openly — with timeline and budget impact noted
- I flag when new requests would push the launch date
- We re-align before adding work, not after

## Why this matters

Endless revisions kill momentum and quality. Structured feedback keeps the project moving, respects everyone's time, and gets you to a result you are proud of — on schedule.
