I prioritize by impact: the screens tied to activation, conversion, and daily usage get designed first. This keeps projects lean and focused on outcomes instead of polishing edge cases before the core flow works.

## How screen priority is decided

**Core journey mapping**
- The path from first visit to first meaningful action inside the product
- Decision points where users convert, activate, or drop off
- Screens your team will build and test against first

**High-impact screens designed early**
- Onboarding, signup, and primary dashboard or home views
- Checkout, pricing, or any flow directly tied to revenue
- Settings and account flows users hit frequently

**Secondary screens come later**
- Admin tools, edge-case settings, and rarely used flows
- Empty states and error pages once the happy path is solid
- Marketing pages that depend on finalized product UI patterns

Designing the right screens first means development and testing can start sooner. You also avoid spending budget polishing admin tools before the core user journey is validated. That sequencing keeps early feedback focused on what actually moves the business.
